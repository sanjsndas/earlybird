Questions from the Kitchen

How do I know if my sourdough starter is ready to use?
A ripe starter should be bubbly, domed, and have a slightly sour, yeasty aroma. It should double in size within 4–6 hours of feeding and pass the float test: drop a teaspoon into water — if it floats, it's ready.
Why did my cake sink in the middle?
The usual suspects: overmixing (develops gluten), opening the oven too early (temperature drop), or too much leavening. Also check your oven temperature with an external thermometer — most home ovens run hot or cold.
Can I use active dry yeast instead of instant?
Yes. Active dry yeast needs to be dissolved in warm liquid (110°F) for 10 minutes before use. Use about 25% more active dry than instant in a recipe. Instant yeast can be added directly to dry ingredients.
What's the best flour for beginner bread bakers?
Bread flour (12–14% protein) is easiest for structure. All-purpose works but absorbs less water. Start with a reliable brand and adjust hydration as you learn. Stone-ground whole wheat adds flavor but needs more water and gentler handling.

About Early Bird Bakes

Early Bird Bakes started as a messy notebook of sourdough experiments and hand-written notes on pie crust. In 2019, I turned it into a blog so I could stop emailing recipes to friends in all-caps.

I'm Maya Collins — a home baker, former line cook, and unapologetic morning person. I believe good baking is less about perfection and more about paying attention: to the feel of dough, the smell of butter browning, the sound of a crust crackling as it cools.

Every recipe here is developed and tested in my small kitchen in Portland, Oregon. I don't use fancy equipment. I don't pretend that every bake goes perfectly. What you'll find is honest, detailed instructions, plenty of troubleshooting notes, and a deep respect for the craft.

What We Believe

  • Recipes should be clear. No vague instructions, no missing steps.
  • Technique matters more than gadgets. A $5 dough scraper beats a $300 mixer for most tasks.
  • Failure is part of learning. Every baker has made hockey puck cookies. We talk about it.
  • Baking is for everyone. You don't need a marble countertop or a pastry degree.

The Team

It's mostly me. Occasionally my partner, Tom, taste-tests and gives brutally honest feedback ("It's fine" means "not good enough"). My dog, Biscuit, cleans up flour spills.

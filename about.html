About Early Bird Bakes

Honest recipes, kitchen stories, and a whole lot of flour — from my small home kitchen to yours.

I'm Maya Collins. I've been baking bread since 2014, writing about it since 2019, and burning things regularly the entire time.

— Maya

The short version

Early Bird Bakes is a one-woman blog about bread, pastry, and the quiet joy of making things with your hands. I started it because I was tired of emailing recipes to friends in all-caps. I kept it going because I fell in love with the conversation — the questions, the troubleshooting, the "I made this and it actually worked" messages.

I believe good baking is less about perfection and more about paying attention: to the feel of dough, the smell of butter browning, the sound of a crust crackling as it cools. My recipes are detailed, tested at least three times, and full of the kind of notes you'd get from a friend who's already made the mistakes so you don't have to.

What you'll find here

  • Recipes that work. Every recipe is developed and tested in a real home kitchen — no professional equipment, no impossible-to-find ingredients.
  • Technique guides. Deep dives into hydration, lamination, fermentation, and all the nerdy stuff that makes baking click.
  • Kitchen stories. The failures, the surprises, the 5 a.m. epiphanies. Baking is a practice, and I write about the whole messy process.
  • No gatekeeping. You don't need a marble countertop or a pastry degree. You need curiosity and a willingness to try again.
“Baking is a form of meditation. The measuring, the mixing, the waiting — it all slows you down. It reminds you that good things take time.”

What I believe

I believe recipes should be clear. I believe technique matters more than gadgets — a $5 dough scraper beats a $300 mixer for most tasks. I believe failure is part of learning, and that every baker has made hockey puck cookies. I believe baking is for everyone.

I also believe in paying attention to the small things: the way a starter smells when it's ready, the exact shade of golden brown on a pie crust, the sound of a good loaf cooling on a rack. These details are what make baking feel like a craft instead of a chore.

The team (it's mostly me)

I develop the recipes, take the photos, write the stories, and eat the results. My partner, Tom, is the official taste-tester — his "it's fine" means "not good enough." My dog, Biscuit, cleans up flour spills and provides emotional support during failed bakes.

Frequently asked questions

Do you use a stand mixer? Rarely. I mix most doughs by hand and use a simple hand mixer for cakes. It's not about the equipment — it's about the technique.

Can I substitute ingredients? Sometimes. I include substitution notes where I've tested them. But baking is chemistry — changing one thing affects everything else. When in doubt, follow the recipe first, then experiment.

Why "Early Bird"? Because I bake in the mornings. It started as a sleep thing and became a ritual. There's something magic about making bread while the rest of the world is still asleep.

Can I share your recipes? Yes! Share links, quote excerpts, tell your friends. Just don't republish full recipes without permission. And always credit Early Bird Bakes.

Let's stay in touch

I send a newsletter every Saturday — one new recipe, a technique deep-dive, and something I burned that week. It's free, it's honest, and you can unsubscribe anytime.

Ready to bake something?

Start with a recipe, or just say hello. I love hearing from people who've made something from the blog.
